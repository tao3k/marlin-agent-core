* Proposed Event Bus contract

This is a design, not an installed bus or an OpenTelemetry exporter. Rust owns
typed event/resource identifiers and lifecycle states. Runtime owns process and
task handles; event delivery never proves that the OS has reclaimed a resource.
A future independent bus replaces overlapping delivery paths after migration.

Reliable publication reserves one item and its accounted bytes at every active
subscriber atomically. If any subscriber lacks capacity, no delivery occurs and
publication reports backpressure; the caller decides whether and how long to
wait. Production accounting must derive retained bytes from owned payloads,
not trust a publisher-supplied number. Oversize and no-subscriber outcomes are explicit. A reservation remains
with each queued event until consumption or subscriber close. Subscriber close
releases queued reservations; it is not an acknowledgement or a durable receipt.
FIFO per subscriber is required. Cancellation before admission leaves no
reservation; cancellation after admission does not retract delivered events.
Shutdown closes admission and specifies whether outstanding queues drain or are
explicitly abandoned. Durable delivery, cross-process ordering and retry dedupe
are not implied. Telemetry consumers must choose a separate bounded loss policy
or isolation; a slow observer must not invisibly block critical state progress.

The Rust =event_bus_contract.rs= is a sequential admission prototype; the Quint
model bounds each of two subscribers to one item and fixes event size, so it
does not prove arbitrary byte sizes, concurrent atomicity or fairness. They are
authored from the SysML requirements; no automatic SysML-to-code generation is
claimed. Production concurrency and subscriber failure remain to be tested.
